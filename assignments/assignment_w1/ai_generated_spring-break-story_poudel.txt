Spring break began right after the end of a long and busy winter quarter, and I was ready to relax and enjoy my time. On the last day of the quarter, I went to the beach with my friends and played volleyball. The weather was warm, and the ocean breeze made everything feel calm and refreshing. We laughed, ran across the sand, and enjoyed the moment without thinking about assignments or deadlines. It felt like the perfect way to close the quarter and begin a break I had been looking forward to for weeks.

However, the next morning did not go as I had expected. I woke up with pain in my right wrist. It was swollen, stiff, and difficult to move. At first, I thought it was just soreness from playing volleyball, but the pain kept getting worse throughout the morning. I became concerned and decided to go to emergency care. The doctor examined my wrist carefully and told me that two tendons in my right hand were inflamed. I felt worried, especially because I had plans for the rest of my break. Even so, I decided to stay positive and not let the injury ruin my experience.

The next day, I went to Channel Islands National Park for a camping trip with my cohort. It was a three-day, two-night trip, and we traveled to and from the island by ferry. Even though my wrist still hurt, I did not want to miss the opportunity. Since I come from a landlocked country, being near the ocean feels especially meaningful to me. As we arrived, I noticed how green and vibrant everything looked in the spring season. The open views, clean air, and steady sound of the ocean made me feel peaceful and excited.

On the first day at the island, we explored the area near our campsite and gradually got used to our surroundings. We walked through green fields and along the coastline, taking in the scenery around us. At one point, I spotted a small island fox, a completely new experience for me. It moved quickly and quietly through the bushes, but seeing wildlife in its natural habitat was genuinely exciting. Later, we went to the beach and spent the afternoon lying on the sand, listening to the waves, and talking with one another. It was calm and unhurried, and it helped me release the stress built up during the previous quarter.
The second day was the most physically demanding. We hiked from our campsite to Smugglers Cove, covering roughly fourteen miles in total. Despite my wrist still causing discomfort, I was determined to keep up with the group. Along the way, we stopped at one of the highest points on the island and shared lunch while taking in the view. The ocean stretched far into the distance, and rolling hills surrounded us in every direction. The scenery made every step feel worthwhile.

Parts of the trail were poorly maintained, which made the hike harder and required careful attention. We navigated narrow, uneven, and occasionally rocky paths with steady focus. When we finally reached Smugglers Cove, we were exhausted but proud. We rested on the beach, ate snacks, drank water, and let our bodies recover. The quiet of that cove made it a moment I know I will carry with me for a long time. The return hike tested us further. Darkness fell, and a light mist settled over the trail. We relied on our phones and headlamps to find our way. Despite the fatigue, we stayed together as a group and kept moving steadily forward. Reaching the campsite that night brought a deep sense of relief, pride, and shared accomplishment that felt hard-earned and genuine. We cooked dinner in the dark using our headlamps for light. The meal was simple, but after such a long day, it tasted better than almost anything. We sat together, recounted the day's events, and laughed about the small obstacles we had faced. Those quiet moments around the campsite, tired and satisfied, made the entire experience feel complete and worthwhile.

On the third day, we went kayaking, which became one of my favorite parts of the trip. Paddling through the water, we passed over a kelp forest growing beneath the surface. The plants swayed with the current, creating a tranquil scene below our kayaks. We also spotted a large seal resting on a nearby beach, which was another moment I found genuinely memorable. After kayaking, we relaxed on the shore before beginning to pack up and prepare for departure. We cleaned the campsite thoroughly, gathered our belongings, and boarded the ferry back to the mainland. The crossing was calm, and I used the time to reflect on everything we had experienced over those three days. I felt grateful that I had decided to go despite my injury, and I returned to the mainland with a quiet sense of fulfillment.

After the camping trip, I spent the remainder of spring break at a slower, more restorative pace. I took daily walks and visited the beach regularly to enjoy the fresh air and open space. One afternoon, I went to Carpinteria City Beach, walked along the shoreline, and collected a few small shells and some sand as simple souvenirs from the week. I also used part of the break to prepare for the upcoming academic term. I did meal prep, organized my schedule, and reviewed my priorities for the weeks ahead. At the same time, I gave myself permission to rest fully watching films, sleeping in, and embracing a quieter rhythm. After a demanding winter quarter, that balance was exactly what I needed.

Looking back, my spring break was an honest mix of difficulty and genuine joy. A wrist injury, a demanding hike, and a night trek through mist were not what I had originally planned. But each challenge gave me something in return. I came home feeling rested, grateful, and ready to begin the new quarter with renewed energy and a clearer perspective.
